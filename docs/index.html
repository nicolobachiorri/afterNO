what happens after you reject cookies

You said NO.
Then what?

afterNO is a browser extension for Chrome and Firefox. You click "Reject" on a site's cookie banner, open afterNO, and it tells you in plain words who can still recognise you. Try it below, on a made-up site.

posso fidarmi?

Non devi crederci
sulla parola.

afterNO non ti chiede fiducia: ti dice da dove viene ogni frase, cosa non può vedere, e quanto spesso ha ragione. Tutto è controllabile.

Provala prima, sul sito inventato

cosa fa

  • Legge la tua scelta: dal segnale di consenso del sito o dal pulsante che hai cliccato.
  • Ti dice quali altre aziende possono ancora riconoscerti su quel sito.
  • Spiega ogni cookie: di chi è, quanto dura, a cosa serve secondo il database.
  • Dichiara la fonte di ogni frase: osservato, possibile o classificato.

cosa non fa

  • Non blocca nulla: osserva e racconta.
  • Non clicca i banner al posto tuo: la scelta resta tua.
  • Non dice mai «questo sito viola la legge»: dice cosa vede, con prudenza.
  • Non si apre da sola: mostra qualcosa solo quando lo chiedi tu.

cosa non può vedere

Un'estensione vede quello che vede il browser. Non vede, e non finge di vedere:

  • il riconoscimento senza cookie (l'impronta del browser, o fingerprinting);
  • gli altri spazi di memoria del browser (localStorage, IndexedDB);
  • i dati che le aziende si passano tra i loro server;
  • cosa fa davvero un'azienda con un cookie: lo scopo è dichiarato, non osservabile.

Ogni frase dice da dove viene.

perché open source

Chi legge i tuoi siti
deve farsi leggere.

Un'estensione che può vedere i cookie di ogni sito che visiti chiede molta fiducia. L'unico modo onesto di chiederla è permettere a chiunque di controllare cosa fa.

aperta

Il codice è pubblico, sotto licenza Apache 2.0.

Chiunque può leggerlo, verificare che non mandi nulla fuori dal browser, costruirla da sé e confrontarla con quella degli store. Anche il metodo di misura e tutti i dati delle misure sono pubblici.

non commerciale

Niente da vendere, niente da raccogliere.

Non ci sono pubblicità, abbonamenti o versioni a pagamento, e non raccogliamo dati: non avremmo dove metterli. È un progetto personale, nato per curiosità e utilità.

i dati dei cookie

Open Cookie Database.

I nomi e le descrizioni dei cookie vengono dall'Open Cookie Database, anch'esso con licenza Apache 2.0, convertito e aggiornato ogni mese in automatico. Le poche correzioni locali sono pubbliche, ciascuna con il suo motivo.

stato

In sviluppo, non ancora negli store.

Funziona su Chrome e Firefox. La pubblicazione negli store arriverà dopo le ultime verifiche.

can I trust it?

You don't have to
take our word.

afterNO doesn't ask for your trust. It tells you where each sentence comes from, what it cannot see, and how often it is right. All of it can be checked.

Try it first, on the made-up site

what it does

  • Reads your choice: from the site's consent signal, or from the button you clicked.
  • Tells you which other companies can still recognise you on that site.
  • Explains every cookie: whose it is, how long it lasts, what it is for according to the database.
  • States the source of every sentence: observed, possible or classified.

what it doesn't do

  • It blocks nothing: it watches and reports.
  • It doesn't click banners for you: the choice stays yours.
  • It never says "this site breaks the law": it says what it sees, carefully.
  • It never opens by itself: it shows something only when you ask.

privacy

Everything stays in your browser.

0 serversNo network requests of its own. The cookie database ships inside the extension.
0 accountsNo sign-up, no login, nothing to connect.
0 analyticsWe don't know who uses it or where: we collect nothing.
goneWhat it remembers is deleted when you close the browser.

what it cannot see

An extension sees what the browser sees. It doesn't see, and doesn't pretend to see:

  • recognition without cookies (browser fingerprinting);
  • other browser storage (localStorage, IndexedDB);
  • data that companies pass between their own servers;
  • what a company actually does with a cookie: the purpose is declared, not observable.

how accurate it is

Measured on 120 sites it had never seen.

Sites drawn at random from a public list, with a method written down before any result. A robot clicks "Reject", saves the verdict and, separately, all the raw data. Two judges from different companies check whether the verdict is right.

coverageOf 40 sites, how many had a free rejection to check. Where there is no rejection, there is nothing to check.
accuracyOf those sites, how many conclusions were right. "All right" on a few sites means "at least so many %", not "never wrong".
stabilityThe same sites visited a second time, with the same version: is the answer the same?

  • The robot uses a fresh browser for every site: the measurement says whether the extension reads the data in front of it correctly, not how often a real user will see "only partly".

three levels of certainty

Every sentence says where it comes from.

observedThe site's consent signal records your rejection

What the browser sees: cookies, contacted sites, the TCF signal.

possible2 third-party sites can recognise you

A technical capability (a long-lived cookie sent back to a company), not proof that it is used.

classified1 cookie is classified as marketing

What the database says, compiled mostly from what companies declare.

The main question, whether anyone can still recognise you after the rejection, rests on the first two levels. The database only adds names and descriptions.

why open source

What reads your sites
should be readable.

An extension that can see the cookies of every site you visit asks for a lot of trust. The only honest way to ask for it is to let anyone check what it does.

open

The code is public, under the Apache 2.0 licence.

Anyone can read it, check that it sends nothing out of the browser, build it themselves and compare it with the store version. The measurement method and all its data are public too.

non-commercial

Nothing to sell, nothing to collect.

No ads, no subscriptions, no paid version, and no data collected: we would have nowhere to put it. It is a personal project, born of curiosity and usefulness.

the cookie data

Open Cookie Database.

Cookie names and descriptions come from the Open Cookie Database, also under Apache 2.0, converted and updated automatically every month. The few local corrections are public, each with its reason.

status

In development, not in the stores yet.

It works on Chrome and Firefox. It will be published in the stores after the last checks.

contact

Write to us.

Reports, a site where it gets things wrong, questions about the method: all welcome.
